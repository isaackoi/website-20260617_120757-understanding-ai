## Introduction

Navigation apps and AI-powered route planners can analyse vast amounts of traffic data, predict delays and suggest efficient routes. However, they do not have legal authority over the road network. When a navigation app conflicts with a road sign, a temporary traffic control, or an instruction from a police officer or other authorised official, the official direction takes priority. Drivers remain legally [responsible]({{ 'responsible-ai/' | relative_url }}) for following the rules that govern the road they are actually using, regardless of what an app recommends. [Understanding]({{ 'understanding/' | relative_url }}) this hierarchy is an important lesson in understanding artificial intelligence: AI can advise, but it does not replace lawful human authority or real-world conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-highway-code/traffic-signs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Highway Code</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Traffic signs - Guidance1 Oct 2015 — Traffic signs used, including signs giving orders, warning signs, direction signs, information signs...</span></span></span>

## Authority of Road Signs

Road signs are part of an official traffic control system established by law and maintained by public authorities. In the United Kingdom, many signs derive their legal force from the Traffic Signs Regulations and General Directions and are reflected in the rules and guidance of the Highway Code. Certain signs communicate legal requirements rather than optional advice. Ignoring them can result in penalties, points on a licence, or more serious consequences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/362/contents" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: legislation.gov.uk">[Legislation.gov.uk+2THE HIGHWAY CODE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">legislation.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Traffic Signs Regulations and General Directions 2016Road markings and miscellaneous upright signs that indicate stopping, waiting, l...</span></span></span>

[Navigation]({{ 'routes/' | relative_url }}) systems operate differently. They generate recommendations based on map data, traffic information and predictive models. Even when highly accurate, those recommendations are informational rather than regulatory. A route suggestion cannot create a legal right to drive the wrong way down a one-way street, enter a restricted zone, exceed a [speed]({{ 'speed/' | relative_url }}) limit or ignore a prohibition sign. The legal authority remains with the official traffic control device visible on the road.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-highway-code/traffic-signs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK+2THE HIGHWAY CODE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Highway Code</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Traffic signs - Guidance1 Oct 2015 — Traffic signs used, including signs giving orders, warning signs, direction signs, information signs...</span></span></span>

This distinction reflects a broader principle in AI governance. AI systems can support [decisions]({{ 'decisions/' | relative_url }}), but they do not automatically inherit the authority of institutions, laws or public officials.

## Why Real Roads Change Faster Than Maps

One reason road signs outrank navigation instructions is that the physical road environment can change immediately, while digital maps require updates.

A navigation app may rely on information collected minutes, hours or even days earlier. By contrast, a newly installed sign is effective as soon as it is lawfully placed. Examples include:

* New speed restrictions.
* Weight or height limits.
* Temporary road closures.
* Diversion routes.
* Access restrictions for events or emergencies.
* Changes caused by construction projects.

Even sophisticated mapping services acknowledge that routing information can change and that users must still observe local conditions and laws.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://support.google.com/waze/answer/12373727?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: support.google.com">[Google Help]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">support.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Help Terms of Use</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google HelpTerms of Use - Waze HelpMay 17, 2018 — These Terms of Use (“Terms”) govern your rights and obligations regarding the use of Wa...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 17, 2018</span></span></span>

## Temporary Traffic Controls Override Normal Routing

### Roadworks and Emergency Management

Temporary traffic controls are among the clearest examples of why official instructions take precedence.

Roadworks often involve portable signs, temporary speed limits, lane closures, barriers and diversion routes. These measures are installed to protect workers and road users in conditions that may not yet appear in digital mapping databases. Official traffic-control standards in multiple countries devote extensive guidance to work-zone management because temporary hazards require immediate and authoritative control of traffic. ### Directions From Police and Authorised Officials

The hierarchy becomes even clearer when a police officer, traffic officer or other authorised person directs traffic.

In many traffic systems, instructions from authorised officials override ordinary traffic rules, signs and signals because they are responding to immediate conditions such as accidents, emergencies, major events or dangerous congestion. ## Driver Responsibility and Liability

A common misconception is that responsibility shifts to the navigation provider when a driver follows incorrect guidance. In practice, drivers generally remain responsible for operating their vehicles lawfully and safely.

Navigation services provide assistance, not legal authorisation. Their terms and policies typically place responsibility for compliance with traffic laws on the user. Drivers are expected to obey road signs, traffic controls and safety requirements regardless of what a routing system displays.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://support.google.com/waze/answer/12373727?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: support.google.com">[Google Help]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">support.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Help Terms of Use</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google HelpTerms of Use - Waze HelpMay 17, 2018 — These Terms of Use (“Terms”) govern your rights and obligations regarding the use of Wa...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 17, 2018</span></span></span>

This matters because liability is attached to the driver's actions in the real world. If a driver enters a prohibited road because an app suggested it, enforcement authorities normally assess the driver's conduct against the road rules and signage in force at that location. The app's recommendation does not cancel those obligations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.highwaycodeuk.co.uk/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: highwaycodeuk.co.uk">[THE HIGHWAY CODE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">highwaycodeuk.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">THE HIGHWAY CODETHE HIGHWAY CODE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK Highway Code 2025 – Free PDF...The aim of The Highway Code is to promote safety on the road, whilst also supporting a healthy, sustai...</span></span></span>

The principle is consistent with many other AI applications. A system can provide recommendations, forecasts or [predictions]({{ 'predictions/' | relative_url }}), but accountability often remains with the human decision-maker.

## What This Reveals About AI Decision Support

Navigation software is a useful example of how artificial intelligence should be understood more generally. AI systems excel at processing large datasets and identifying patterns that humans might miss. They can often recommend faster or more efficient routes than a person could plan alone.

Yet the system's authority is limited. Road signs, temporary controls and official instructions represent legally recognised decisions made by public authorities. AI recommendations are advisory and must be evaluated against those real-world controls.

When drivers understand that hierarchy, they are using AI as intended: as a decision-support tool rather than an unquestionable source of authority.
