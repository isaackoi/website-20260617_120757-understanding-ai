## Introduction

As AI image and video editing becomes more sophisticated, the question is increasingly shifting from “Does this look fake?” to “Can its history be verified?”. Provenance systems are designed to answer that second question. Rather than analysing pixels for signs of manipulation, they attach a verifiable record showing where a piece of media came from, who created or edited it, and what tools were involved. In the context of [synthetic]({{ 'synthetic-media/' | relative_url }}) images and videos, provenance records offer a different approach to information integrity: they aim to document the chain of custody of digital media rather than guess whether content is authentic from appearance alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://c2pa.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: c2pa.org">[C2PA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">c2pa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">C2PA &#124; Verifying Media Content SourcesExplore a global content provenance and authenticity standard called Content Credentials. The most influential current effort is the Content Credentials ecosystem built around the Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity (C2PA) standard. Support has expanded across technology firms, camera manufacturers, media organisations and AI developers, but adoption remains uneven and important limitations remain. Provenance can strengthen transparency, yet it does not automatically prove that a picture is true or that every image online is covered by the system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://c2pa.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: c2pa.org">[C2PA+2C2PA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">c2pa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">C2PA &#124; Verifying Media Content SourcesExplore a global content provenance and authenticity standard called Content Credentials. ## Can Content Credentials Restore Trust in Images?

### What provenance records try to prove

A provenance record is a structured history attached to a media file. It may include information such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The organisation or device that created the image or video.
* The date and time of creation.
* Editing actions performed later.
* Whether AI tools were used during creation or modification.
* Digital signatures linking the record to a trusted source.

</div>

The key innovation is that these records are cryptographically signed. If someone alters the protected parts of the file after the record is created, the signature can fail [validation]({{ 'stop-training/' | relative_url }}), indicating that the provenance trail has been disrupted. This makes provenance systems tamper-evident rather than merely descriptive. In practice, Content Credentials function somewhat like a nutrition label for media. Instead of asking viewers to determine whether an image is AI-generated by visual inspection, the system allows them to inspect information about origin, editing history and authorship. Adobe, Google, OpenAI and other participants have promoted this approach as a way to increase transparency around AI-generated and AI-edited content.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2024/09/18/authenticity-age-ai-growing-content-credentials-momentum-across-social-media-platforms-ai-companies-rising-consumer-awareness" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blog.adobe.com">[Adobe Blog+2blog.google]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blog.adobe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Adobe BlogAuthenticity in the Age of AI: Growing Content Credentials...18 Sept 2024 — Content Credentials are essentially a “nutrition l...</span></span></span>

A useful example is photojournalism. New provenance-enabled camera systems can record information at the moment a photograph is captured and preserve a chain of edits through publication. Canon's 2026 authenticity system for news organisations is designed around this principle, embedding provenance information from capture through editorial workflows so that recipients can verify where an image originated and how it changed over time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.digitalcameraworld.com/photography/photojournalism/canon-rolls-out-c2pa-compliant-image-verification-for-professional-newsrooms" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitalcameraworld.com">[Digital Camera World]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitalcameraworld.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Officially launching in May 2026 across Europe, the Middle East, and Africa, the system is initially compatible with Canon&#x27;s EOS R1 and E...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2026</span></span></span>

### Why provenance differs from AI detection

AI detection systems attempt to infer whether content was generated or manipulated. Provenance systems instead document actions performed by participating tools and organisations.

This distinction matters because detection technologies often struggle as image generators improve. Provenance records do not need to recognise visual artefacts. Instead, they answer questions such as:

* Which software created or edited the file?
* Was [generative AI]({{ 'generative-ai/' | relative_url }}) involved?
* Which organisation signed the record?
* Has the file been modified since the record was issued?

If widely adopted, provenance shifts [verification]({{ 'verification-cost/' | relative_url }}) away from subjective visual judgement and towards inspectable [documentation]({{ 'paper-safety/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://c2pa.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: c2pa.org">[C2PA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">c2pa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">C2PA &#124; Verifying Media Content SourcesExplore a global content provenance and authenticity standard called Content Credentials. Conte...</span></span></span>

However, provenance is strongest when media originates within systems that actively create and preserve records. It is not a universal detector for every image circulating online.

## Why Metadata and Signatures Can Break Down

The most common weakness of provenance systems is that the information can disappear before viewers ever see it.

Many online platforms routinely remove embedded metadata when users upload images and videos. Historically this process was unrelated to AI and was done for storage, privacy or performance reasons. Unfortunately, provenance information often travels alongside the same metadata and can be stripped out as well. Researchers and industry participants have repeatedly identified metadata removal as a major obstacle to large-scale deployment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://research.adobe.com/news/content-authenticity-and-image-fingerprinting-qa-with-john-collomosse/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: research.adobe.com">[Adobe Research+2SoftwareSeni]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">research.adobe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Adobe ResearchContent Authenticity and Image Fingerprinting4 Apr 2023 — One of the challenges is that the metadata can be stripped away f...</span></span></span>

Developers have responded by experimenting with more durable approaches. Some systems maintain cloud-based copies of credentials, while others combine metadata with watermarking or fingerprinting techniques intended to survive screenshots, recompression and other transformations. These approaches seek to preserve provenance even when the original file metadata is lost.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://helpx.adobe.com/creative-cloud/apps/adobe-content-authenticity/content-credentials/manage-content-credentials.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: helpx.adobe.com">[Adobe Help Center+2Content Authenticity Initiative]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">helpx.adobe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Adobe Help CenterManage Content Credentials5 days ago — This makes your Content Credentials more durable, ensuring they are recoverable e...</span></span></span>

Another limitation is coverage. Provenance records can only document actions that participating tools, platforms or devices choose to record. An image generated by an [unsupported]({{ 'unsupported-claims/' | relative_url }}) tool, edited offline, or deliberately stripped of credentials may circulate without any provenance information at all. The absence of a provenance record therefore does not necessarily prove that content is fake. It may simply indicate that the content originated outside participating systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://c2pa.org/faqs/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: c2pa.org">[C2PA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">c2pa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FAQsIn contrast, Content Credentials are about transparency and integrity—they record and preserve the provenance of digital media wi...</span></span></span>

### Provenance does not prove truth

A common misunderstanding is that provenance systems certify that an image is accurate.

In reality, they primarily certify the history attached to a file. A valid provenance record may show who created content and how it was edited, but it does not independently verify that the depicted event actually occurred. An image can carry authentic provenance information and still contain misleading claims, staged scenes or incorrect contextual descriptions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://c2pa.org/faqs/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: c2pa.org">[C2PA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">c2pa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FAQsIn contrast, Content Credentials are about transparency and integrity—they record and preserve the provenance of digital media wi...</span></span></span>

This distinction is particularly important in journalism and public policy. Provenance can improve transparency about a media asset's origin, yet factual verification still requires traditional reporting, corroborating evidence and source evaluation.

### Security and governance concerns

Researchers have also raised concerns about whether current provenance standards fully achieve all of their intended security goals. Recent academic analyses argue that provenance systems remain promising but should not yet be treated as definitive evidence in the highest-stakes contexts without additional safeguards and validation procedures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24890" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Verifying Provenance of Digital Media: Why the C2PA Specifications Fall ShortApril 27, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 27, 2026</span></span></span>

Other research has highlighted potential inconsistencies between provenance metadata and separate watermarking systems. In some circumstances, a file may present conflicting signals about whether AI was involved, illustrating the complexity of building reliable verification ecosystems across multiple technologies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02378" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Authenticated Contradictions from Desynchronized Provenance and WatermarkingMarch 2, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 2, 2026</span></span></span>

## How Readers Can Use Provenance Alongside Corroboration

For ordinary users, provenance is most valuable as an additional verification layer rather than a standalone truth test.

When provenance information is available, readers can ask several practical questions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. **Who signed the record?** A recognised news organisation, camera manufacturer or AI provider may carry more credibility than an unknown source.
2. **Does the edit history make sense?** Provenance can reveal whether AI tools were used and whether significant modifications occurred.
3. **Is the signature still valid?** Broken or missing signatures may indicate that the media has been altered after the record was created.
4. **Do independent sources confirm the event?** Provenance should be combined with corroborating reporting, eyewitness accounts and additional evidence.

</div>

This approach mirrors how people evaluate documents, photographs and news reports in other contexts. Provenance contributes information about origin and handling, while corroboration addresses whether the underlying claims are accurate.
