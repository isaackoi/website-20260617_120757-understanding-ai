## Introduction

Before a chatbot can answer a question, it must convert the text into units it can process. These units are called tokens. A token may be a whole word, part of a word, punctuation, or even a space combined with nearby characters. As a result, a language model does not begin with the same building blocks that a human reader sees on the screen. The way text is divided into tokens influences what patterns the model learns during training and how it generates answers later. Research increasingly shows that tokenisation is not merely a technical pre-processing step; it can affect reasoning, [multilingual]({{ 'language-bias/' | relative_url }}) performance, spelling behaviour, efficiency, and even the probability of particular responses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/ai/conceptual/understanding-tokens" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: learn.microsoft.com">[Microsoft Learn+2Clearbox AI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">learn.microsoft.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Learn Understanding tokens</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft LearnUnderstanding tokens -.NETMar 13, 2026 — Understand how large language models (LLMs) use tokens to analyze semantic relat...</span></span></span>

## Why chatbots do not really read words

Large [language models]({{ 'language-models/' | relative_url }}) work with sequences of token IDs rather than written words. The tokenizer converts text into numerical identifiers, and the model processes those identifiers throughout training and generation. Only after the model has produced a sequence of output tokens are those tokens converted back into readable text.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/ai/conceptual/understanding-tokens" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: learn.microsoft.com">[Microsoft Learn+2Clearbox AI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">learn.microsoft.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Learn Understanding tokens</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft LearnUnderstanding tokens -.NETMar 13, 2026 — Understand how large language models (LLMs) use tokens to analyze semantic relat...</span></span></span>

This means that the apparent word structure seen by a user is not necessarily the structure seen by the model. A common word might be represented as a single token, while a rare word could be split into several pieces. For example, a tokenizer may store a frequent word as one unit but represent an unusual technical term as multiple fragments. Modern GPT-style systems commonly use variants of Byte Pair Encoding (BPE), which build vocabularies from frequently occurring character patterns rather than from complete dictionary words.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huggingface.co/learn/llm-course/en/chapter6/5" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huggingface.co">[Hugging Face+2Sebastian Raschka, PhD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huggingface.co</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hugging FaceByte-Pair Encoding tokenizationByte-Pair Encoding (BPE) was initially developed as an algorithm to compress texts, and then u...</span></span></span>

The practical consequence is that two phrases with nearly identical meanings may be represented internally in very different ways. One version may align neatly with familiar tokens, while another may be fragmented into many smaller pieces. The model's [predictions]({{ 'predictions/' | relative_url }}) are therefore shaped partly by how easily the tokenizer can represent the text.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21933" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Effect of Tokenization on Performance of LLMsDecember 26, 2025 — by S Pawar · 2025 · Cited by 2 — In this paper, we hypothesize that...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 26, 2025</span></span></span>

## What tokens are and why they vary

Token boundaries are uneven because tokenizers are designed to balance vocabulary size against efficiency. Frequent patterns receive their own tokens, while uncommon patterns are assembled from smaller pieces. On average, a token often corresponds to only a few bytes of text rather than a complete word.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://github.com/openai/tiktoken" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: github.com">[GitHub]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">github.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">tiktoken is a fast BPE tokeniser for use with OpenAI&#x27;s models.It&#x27;s reversible and lossless, so you can convert tokens back into the...</span></span></span>

## How token IDs and embeddings carry patterns

After tokenisation, each token is mapped to a numerical ID. Those IDs are then transformed into embeddings, which are mathematical vectors that capture statistical relationships learned during training. Tokens that often appear in similar contexts tend to develop related representations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/ai/conceptual/understanding-tokens" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: learn.microsoft.com">[Microsoft Learn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">learn.microsoft.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Learn Understanding tokens</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft LearnUnderstanding tokens -.NETMar 13, 2026 — Understand how large language models (LLMs) use tokens to analyze semantic relat...</span></span></span>

Because embeddings are attached to tokens rather than directly to words, the model's [understanding]({{ 'understanding/' | relative_url }}) is partly shaped by the tokenizer's decisions. If a concept is represented by a stable token or token sequence, the model can accumulate rich statistical knowledge about it. If the same concept is frequently fragmented into different token combinations, learning may be less efficient. Research examining tokenisation quality has found measurable relationships between token fragmentation and downstream model performance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21933" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv+2ACL Anthology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Effect of Tokenization on Performance of LLMsDecember 26, 2025 — by S Pawar · 2025 · Cited by 2 — In this paper, we hypothesize that...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 26, 2025</span></span></span>

This helps explain why tokenisation is often described as part of the model's representational system rather than merely a storage format. The tokenizer determines the pieces from which all later patterns are built.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.clearbox.ai/blog/quick-introduction-tokenizers" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: clearbox.ai">[Clearbox AI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">clearbox.ai</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AIA quick</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A quick introduction to tokenizersIt&#x27;s important to note that tokenization is always performed before training a language model. ## Where token boundaries can change outputs

One of the most surprising findings in recent research is that token boundaries can influence the probability of generated text even when the visible wording appears almost identical.

Whitespace provides a simple example. Some tokenizers treat a word with a leading space as a different token from the same word without that space. Researchers have shown that such distinctions can alter the probabilities assigned to subsequent tokens, subtly changing generation behaviour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11339515/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">This behavior introduces subtle biases that affect the model&#x27;s language generation process and response characteristics...</span></span></span>

More dramatic effects appear when prompts end at [awkward token]({{ 'token-splits/' | relative_url }}) boundaries. Recent studies describe a "partial token" problem in which a prompt may end in the middle of what the tokenizer expects to be a larger token. In these cases, models can assign dramatically different probabilities to continuations compared with equivalent prompts aligned to token boundaries. The effect has been observed across leading language models and can be especially important in code, highly compounding languages, and scripts that do not rely on spaces between words.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.23223" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Are you going to finish that? Researchers have also documented broader forms of tokenisation bias. Because some languages, writing systems, and word structures are segmented more efficiently than others, models may devote different amounts of representational capacity to similar meanings. These differences can affect performance across languages and tasks.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17179" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv+2Emergent Mind]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span></span></span>

## Why tokenisation remains an active area of research

Tokenisation was once treated as a largely solved engineering problem. Increasingly, researchers view it as a factor that can shape model behaviour in ways that remain poorly understood. Studies have linked tokenizer choice to downstream task performance, multilingual fairness, reasoning consistency, and sensitivity to prompt formatting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://huggingface.co/papers/2512.20757" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: huggingface.co">[Hugging Face+2OpenReview]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">huggingface.co</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hugging FaceTokSuite: Measuring the Impact of Tokenizer Choice on...Dec 23, 2025 — TokSuite enables systematic研究 of tokenization effects...</span></span></span>

Other research focuses on improving token alignment, reducing tokenisation bias, or designing tokenizers that better match linguistic structure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09303" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv+2Emergent Mind]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span></span></span>
