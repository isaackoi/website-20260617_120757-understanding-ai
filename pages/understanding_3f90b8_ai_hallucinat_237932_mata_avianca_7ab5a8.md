## Introduction

*Mata v. Avianca* became the most famous early example of an AI hallucination causing real-world professional consequences. In 2023, lawyers representing a passenger in a lawsuit against Avianca Airlines submitted a court filing that cited multiple legal cases which did not exist. The citations had been generated by ChatGPT and were accepted by the lawyers without proper [verification]({{ 'verification-cost/' | relative_url }}). When the court investigated, it discovered that the cases were fictional. The incident led to sanctions against the attorneys and quickly became a defining warning about the risks of relying on [generative AI]({{ 'generative-ai/' | relative_url }}) for factual research.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/new-york/nysdce/1%3A2022cv01461/575368/55/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.justia.com">[Justia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.justia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mata v. Avianca, Inc., No. 1:2022cv01461 - Document 55 (S.D.N.Y. 2023):: JustiaJune 22, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 22, 2023</span></span></span>

## What went wrong in the filing

The underlying lawsuit involved claims brought by passenger Roberto Mata against Avianca. During briefing on a procedural issue, plaintiff's counsel submitted a filing that relied on several supposed judicial [decisions]({{ 'decisions/' | relative_url }}). Opposing lawyers and the court were unable to locate those authorities in legal databases, triggering further scrutiny.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/new-york/nysdce/1%3A2022cv01461/575368/55/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.justia.com">[Justia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.justia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mata v. Avianca, Inc., No. 1:2022cv01461 - Document 55 (S.D.N.Y. 2023):: JustiaJune 22, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 22, 2023</span></span></span>

The investigation revealed that attorney Steven Schwartz had used ChatGPT to help conduct legal research. The system generated citations to cases such as *Varghese v. China Southern Airlines*, *Shaboon v. Egyptair* and *Petersen v. Iran Air*. These cases were not merely misquoted or misunderstood; they did not exist at all. ChatGPT also supplied fabricated summaries, quotations and procedural details that made the authorities appear genuine.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://aiwiki.ai/wiki/ai_in_law" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aiwiki.ai">[AI Wiki]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aiwiki.ai</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AI Wiki AI in law &#124; AI Wiki</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AI WikiAI in law &#124; AI WikiMarch 25, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 25, 2026</span></span></span>

A particularly striking aspect of the incident was what happened after the citations were challenged. Rather than independently verifying the authorities through established legal research tools, Schwartz reportedly asked ChatGPT whether the cases were real. The system incorrectly confirmed that they existed and even claimed they could be found in major legal databases. Those assurances were also false.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://responsibleailabs.ai/ai-watch/mata-v-avianca-lawyers-sanctioned-for-chatgpt-fabricated-cases" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: responsibleailabs.ai">[Responsible AI Labs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">responsibleailabs.ai</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on responsibleailabs.ai.</span></span></span>

Judge P. Kevin Castel concluded that the lawyers had failed in their professional duty to verify the authorities they presented to the court. In June 2023, he sanctioned the attorneys and their law firm, imposing monetary penalties and requiring additional corrective actions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ixsor.com/insights/mata-three-years-on" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ixsor.com">[IXSOR+2AI Wiki]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ixsor.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mata v. Avianca, Three Years On · IXSORMay 6, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 6, 2026</span></span></span>

## Why legal hallucinations are unusually risky

Many AI errors are inconvenient. Legal [hallucinations]({{ 'hallucinations/' | relative_url }}) can be much more serious because legal systems depend on verifiable authority. A court filing is expected to cite actual statutes, regulations and judicial opinions. If a lawyer submits invented authorities, judges, opposing parties and clients may all be misled.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.americanbar.org/groups/litigation/resources/newsletters/appellate-practice/ai-hallucinations-real-how-avoid-them/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: americanbar.org">[American Bar Association]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">americanbar.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">American Bar Association AI Hallucinations Are Real—and How to Avoid Them</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">American Bar Association AI Hallucinations Are Real—and How to Avoid Them</span></span></span>

The *Mata* incident exposed several characteristics that make AI-generated legal errors especially dangerous:

* **Authority signals can be fabricated.** The fake cases included realistic case names, citations and quotations, making them appear trustworthy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://aiwiki.ai/wiki/ai_in_law" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aiwiki.ai">[AI Wiki]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aiwiki.ai</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AI Wiki AI in law &#124; AI Wiki</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AI WikiAI in law &#124; AI WikiMarch 25, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 25, 2026</span></span></span>
* **Confidence can disguise uncertainty.** ChatGPT presented the fictional authorities in the same style it would use for genuine cases. Nothing in the text clearly signalled that the information had been invented.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://responsibleailabs.ai/ai-watch/mata-v-avianca-lawyers-sanctioned-for-chatgpt-fabricated-cases" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: responsibleailabs.ai">[Responsible AI Labs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">responsibleailabs.ai</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on responsibleailabs.ai.</span></span></span>
* **Verification shortcuts can fail.** Asking the same AI system to verify its own output simply reproduced the original error rather than detecting it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://responsibleailabs.ai/ai-watch/mata-v-avianca-lawyers-sanctioned-for-chatgpt-fabricated-cases" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: responsibleailabs.ai">[Responsible AI Labs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">responsibleailabs.ai</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on responsibleailabs.ai.</span></span></span>
* **Professional trust is at stake.** Courts rely on lawyers to act as gatekeepers who check the accuracy of submitted material. When that process breaks down, confidence in legal proceedings can be damaged.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.americanbar.org/groups/litigation/resources/newsletters/appellate-practice/ai-hallucinations-real-how-avoid-them/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: americanbar.org">[American Bar Association]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">americanbar.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">American Bar Association AI Hallucinations Are Real—and How to Avoid Them</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">American Bar Association AI Hallucinations Are Real—and How to Avoid Them</span></span></span>

The case therefore illustrated a broader lesson about large language models: fluency and apparent expertise are not evidence that a statement has been verified against reliable sources.

## What the case changed about verification

The immediate impact of *Mata v. Avianca* was educational. Lawyers, judges and legal organisations suddenly had a concrete example of how generative AI could fail. The incident was widely discussed in legal publications, professional training programmes and court guidance documents.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://legal-mag.com/legal-fictions-and-chatgpt-hallucinations-mata-v-avianca-and-generative-ai-in-the-courts/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: legal-mag.com">[Legal Mag]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">legal-mag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Legal MagLegal Fictions and ChatGPT Hallucinations: ‘Mata v. ## The lasting lesson from the Avianca case

The enduring importance of *Mata v. Avianca* is that it transformed the abstract idea of AI hallucination into a concrete, documented event. The problem was not that ChatGPT made a minor mistake. It generated a network of fictional legal authorities that looked authentic enough to enter federal litigation. Human professionals then failed to detect the fabrication before submitting it to a court.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://aiwiki.ai/wiki/ai_in_law" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aiwiki.ai">[AI Wiki]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aiwiki.ai</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AI Wiki AI in law &#124; AI Wiki</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AI WikiAI in law &#124; AI WikiMarch 25, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 25, 2026</span></span></span>
